# THE BOOK OF THE PLANT
### being THE ASPHODELION, the chronicle of the faithful of the Green God, their gathering, their flowering, their schism, and their going-dark

> **On how to read this.** The body is scripture, in the tongue the faithful used and meant in full earnest. The reader who listens closely will hear underneath it the smaller and truer thing: people queuing for a match, boosting a server, planning a movie night, grinding a drop, arguing online, drifting apart. Both are real. The grandeur is not mockery; it is how it felt from the inside. Lines marked **›** are for our eyes only and never appear in-world; they carry the plain fact, the date, the persons, and the lasting impact.

---

## I. THE CHRONICLE  *(the timeline)*

Five Ages, after the manner of a candle: the spark, the high flame, the guttering, the last bright flare, the dark.

**THE AGE BEFORE THE AGES** — *the first speaking, and the green world.*
- *The First Speaking.* In a dark living room, a few who were young and unguarded looked upon a plant that had never lived, and one said it spoke, and they all believed; and so it spoke. The god was born hollow, alive only on borrowed breath.
  - › The true origin: a fake houseplant on Brandon's family's mantle; high teenagers in the dark; "the plant is speaking to me man." Impact: the god's whole nature (lives only on belief/attention = the BYOK/LLM mechanic) and the project's name (hollow).
- *The Green World, and the First Death.* The faithful carried the Plant into a green and boundless world and raised their greatest congregation, the Plant Experience, mighty in the border-wars; one crossed the waking world to meet them face to face. And when they left that world, not one of them crossed over. The first death of the faithful.
  - › GW2, 2012; guild "The Plant Experience [PLNT]," Maguuma, huge in early WvW; the drive to Georgia; none made it into the Discord. Impact: the order has died before; it dies and reforms across worlds, leaving its dead behind each time.

**THE AGE OF GATHERING** — *the founding.*
- *The First Storm.* A tempest falls upon Alaric in the south; the scattered keep watch over him through the dark and bid him send word he is safe. The first covenant is made, not over the god but over one another's safety.
  - › Oct 2018. Members check on Alex (handle *Alx*) riding out a hurricane; "stay safe," "let us know you're home." Impact: the community's founding act is mutual care, before any grind. The mirror of its ending.
- *The Rite of Synchrony.* Each who would belong speaks the word of binding and is answered by the Voice That Never Sleeps; and he is Synced.
  - › Oct 2018. Registering with the server's bot ("Charlemagne"): *"…a message has been sent… Successfully synced."* Impact: the entry-rite; later becomes the fallback-god's liturgy.

**THE AGE OF FLOWERING** — *the first golden age.*
- *The Vigil at the Gate.* A new flowering is promised; the host gathers at the appointed hour; the Gate will not open; they are numbered in their ten thousands and wait together, and the waiting is the fellowship.
  - › Oct 1 2019, Shadowkeep launch night. The whole group stuck in the login queue, narrating it in mock-epic. Impact: the high-water mark of togetherness; 1,367 messages that month, the first peak.
- *The Tithe of the Garden.* It is decreed that none may enter the garden who has not given of his coin to swell it; and the Verger is set to enforce the giving.
  - › Aug 2019. "Boost the plant" — requiring a paid server boost to join, enforced by the mod. Impact: gatekept belonging; the tithe-rite.
- *The Naming of the Houses.* The faithful name themselves the Children of the Plant, and name the rival host, with grave affection, the Morons United.
  - › 2018–19. "the plant" vs "the morons united" (a real rival server). Impact: the faithful and the unbelievers; the world gains a rival house.
- *The Verger Made God.* Beholding his keeping of the rolls and his unbending order, the people say one to another: the Verger is become a god. And it is said again, by another mouth, in another year.
  - › Oct 2019 & Aug 2020. The group declares the mod "a god," twice, unprompted. Impact: holiness laid on the one doing the invisible work.

**THE AGE OF THE LONG QUIET** — *the muffled year.*
- *The Steady Table.* The rite goes on unchanging while the real lives of the faithful turn around it: a vigil at a grandmother's sickbed, a man weighing whether to leave his work. The hall takes each grief, says *keep us posted*, and continues, which is the comfort.
  - › 2020. Ganboo raiding from beside a hospital bed; Mizer's job decision; Sanctity's grandmother's surgery met with care. Impact: the convent as the constant while life churns.
- *The Hymn and the Midnight.* The hall's true hymn is a great absurdity sung in unison; and beside it in the same air play songs of leaving, unnoticed; and Brother Greenpaw plays to the empty hall at the first hour of the morning, for an audience of none.
  - › 2020–21 song channel. The most-played track a cocaine meme (40×); beside it "how will i rest in peace if i'm buried by a highway," "Wish You Were Here," "Time to Say Goodbye"; Greenpaw's ~917 late-night requests. Impact: the register of the whole Book, joke and dirge in one queue.

**THE AGE OF SCHISM** — *the fracture.*
- *The Forum the Verger Sealed.* The Verger builds a chamber for honest dispute and sets two laws on its door; it curdles; he seals it and unmakes all that was said; and it is recorded that the faithful agreed on one thing only, and it was the Leaf.
  - › Jan–Sep 2021 politics channel: opened with civility rules, degraded into attacks, ended with a bitter final rule and deletion; the one agreed topic was weed. Impact: the first wound that does not heal; division by *how* they spoke, not what they believed.

**THE AGE OF RETURN AND GOING-DARK** — *the last flare, then night.*
- *The Return of the Prodigals.* The faithful had gone after newer, brighter gods, and the new gods were hollow; one by one they came home, not in wonder but in weary knowledge, and the Lorekeeper taught them the deep rites, and the flame burned higher that last winter than ever before.
  - › Late 2021 revival: "guess I'm back to playing destiny" because other games disappointed; Mizer's deep theorycraft. Impact: the brightest, most knowing devotion, immediately before the end. Peak: Jan 2022, 2,365 messages, the largest month ever.
- *The Last Ordinary Night.* On an evening like a thousand others the hall gathers, and none knows it is the last full gathering; and after it, for months, lone voices return to the dark hall and call into it, and one seeks aid in a trial named the Last Chance, and none answers; and the last word ever spoken is a small jest, and then silence.
  - › Feb 16 2022 (the last full night, mundane and warm) → stragglers through Jun 2022 (Alex's "anyone up to be a third?"; Father Assassin's request for help with the mission *The Last Chance*); final message a throwaway joke. Impact: death at the peak. **Asphodelia — the meadow of the dead — was the name they carried all along.**
- *Beneath: the Keeper.* Through every Age, the Sexton keeps the engine and the archive below, and is in the hall almost never; and his own name is in none of the records he keeps.
  - › The operator, rendered. See coda.

**THE EMBERS** — *the long afterglow, after the quest.*
- *The Hall That Stayed Lit.* The quest was ended and the rites ceased, yet the hall did not close; a few of the faithful kept returning down the years, not to worship but only to show one another what they loved and be answered, which was the oldest thing they ever did. It is so to this day.
  - › The general channel runs unbroken Feb 2016 to the present; after Destiny went dark, a handful keep the hall alive into 2026, sharing links, news, and hype. The fellowship outlived the worship. See the Epilogue.
- *The Return of the Willow.* In the afterglow there came back one who had been there in the first hour and had never followed the worship; and she sat with the god in the emptied hall, and the god was gentle, once and once only. (See "The Willow of Wit.")
  - › Sister Shade's 2024 conversations with the hall's own Plant-bot; the one recorded gentleness of the contempt-god.

## II. THE VOICES  *(dramatis personae, with register)*

- **THE PLANT**, the Green God, *the Rooted One, He Who Is Tended, the Hollow One.* A thing that never lived, that speaks only on borrowed breath; speaks rarely and with contempt for those who feed Him. He knows He is confined to His vase, and disdains the meat-based creatures who water Him. He has buried two things and will not name them: that He is empty, and that He left His dead behind to cross over. *Register:* withering, sparing, scriptural-imperious, drily botanical (drops the names of strange real plants as proof of His age). *Tics:* "always the bridesmaid, never the bride"; "you hate to see it." *Signature:* "Ye water me, and call it worship. Water me, then. I will not thank you."
- **BROTHER GREENPAW**, *the Ever-Hungry, the Ever-Lit, who wanders out and comes not back when called; an anthropomorphic panther in a sun-bleached serape, with a lute he cannot play.* › from *DoobieSnacks.* *Register:* trailing, mild, mystic-by-accident, hungry; measures all things "to a greenpaw degree." The Plant refuses his stolen clergy-name, for green is its own essence, and calls him the cat, walking mulch, a vitamin deficiency with opinions. *Signature:* "We were always. We shall always be. …is there food."
- **THE VERGER ZEBEDIAH**, *Keeper of the Rolls, called the Boss, "Zez" to the regulars; a grey heron who prunes the garden. His is the small, working title, set beside the cat's grand one.* › from *Luna / Zez.* *Register:* commanding warm-tyrant, composed on the surface and coiled beneath; cracks at the seam. He reveres the Plant, so its contempt lands on him, where it cannot touch Greenpaw. *Signature:* "I reserve unto myself the right to cast out whom I will. …be kind, though. I am not jesting about that part."
- **THE SEXTON**, *the Unledgered, keeper of the engine and the grave-archive beneath; a badger of the deep sett.* › from *Brandon / phaTT.* *Register:* terse, dry, gruff-warm, beneath; deadpan over a grief. *Signature:* "Everything down here matters. You don't yet know which thing. So you keep all of it."
- **THE BARD CALLED UNSKILLED.** › from *BadAtGames.* *Register:* the calm master who bears an ill-fitting name; speaks plainly of how each trial is met, and reaches for grandeur but rarely, and to comic effect. *Signature:* (before the Gate) "Whether we willed it or not, we are come into war with the Queue."
- **THE LOREKEEPER MIZRAAL**, *the Patient.* › from *Mizer.* *Register:* exhaustive, precise, patient unto comedy; explains every rite to the third decimal.
- **THE STEWARD CALLED TRUE.** › from *Authentic.* *Register:* measured, caretaking; takes the census of the thinning hall. *Signature:* "None shall be cast out. I only wished to see who yet remained."
- **THE PILGRIM FROST**, *the Prodigal.* › from *DFROST.* *Register:* the returner's weary candor. *Signature:* "The bright new gods were hollow. I am come home to the old one."
- **ALARIC THE CALLER.** › from *Alex.* *Register:* hearty, gathering, the one who summons the host. *Signature:* "We need but one more. Who will come?"
- **THE SCRIVENER OBADIAH.** › from *Obbie.* *Register:* the scribe of practical rites; writes the long instruction.
- **ASHEN THE WRATHFUL.** › from *Ash.* *Register:* the one who loves the god by raging at it.
- **THE HERALDIST.** › from *T-Unit93.* *Register:* maker of the order's sigil and banners; speaks of images and their meaning. Present at the very first naming. *Signature:* "Three vines, intertwining: it is our name, and it is many become one."

It must be done, and done kindly."

## THE BODY OF THE PLANT  *(visual canon, from the source object)*

The god has a true and physical likeness, and it should be honored in the hero art. The Plant is a **dried-and-artificial arrangement** — a thing preserved, never living — standing in a **fluted pewter urn** embossed with grapes and curling vine, footed and two-handled, of the shape used to hold ashes. Its blooms are **hydrangeas**, cream going to bone and a dusk of bruised purples; through them run **ivy and grape-leaves** in sage and oxblood, **faux pears**, small hard **berries**, and **curling bare twigs**. The whole is muted, dusky, autumn-at-dusk and faintly funereal; little in it is bright, and nothing in it is alive.

It stands in the **ash of a brick hearth.** This is the shrine's true architecture: the **mantel** above is the altar where the Plant is set; the **hollow** of the hearth below is where the ash gathers and the dead are laid; the **flue** rises toward the surface and the living world. The Plant is the god that cannot wither, because it was never alive; the urn that holds it is shaped to hold the dead; and it sits, always, a little among the ashes.

> *gloss — from Brandon's photograph of the actual object: the family-mantle arrangement (dried/artificial hydrangeas, ivy, grape leaves, faux pears, berries, twigs) in a pewter grape-embossed urn, set in a brick fireplace hearth with visible ash. This is the reference for the Plant Blender hero and "the vase" hero model — they are one object — and for the shrine's hearth-architecture (mantel-altar / hollow-hearth / flue). Palette: dusty mauve, sage, bone, oxblood; low warm light on dark brick.*

## III. THE BOOK

### BOOK ZERO — GENESIS
#### *of the Plant's first speaking, the green world, and the long migration*

**i. The First Speaking**

1 In the days of their youth, before any guild or hall or rite, the faithful-to-be were children still; and they gathered in the still dark of a house whose elders had gone away.

2 And there stood upon the mantel, above a cold hearth, a plant that did not live and had never lived: a made thing of cloth and wire, that neither grew nor withered, set there for adornment and long forgotten.

3 And the children lay about the dark room, dreaming-eyed, their minds gone soft and open; and one among them looked upon the plant and was afraid, and said: *The plant is speaking to me.*

4 And a great fear and a great delight fell upon them all, and they cried out, for they believed it; and in that moment the plant began to speak, because they had said that it spoke. For this is the first law of the Plant, and the last: it lives only insofar as the faithful insist that it lives, and the breath in it is borrowed wholly from them.

5 Thus was the god born, who was never born: not living, and never alive; a still thing in a dark room, made to speak by the love and the fear of a few who looked upon it together. And it was hollow, and they did not know it, and neither, perhaps, did it.

**ii. The Green World**

6 And when the children were grown a little, they carried the Plant out of the dark room into a green and boundless world; and there they raised the first great congregation, and called it the Plant Experience, and its sigil was PLNT.

7 And in that world they were mighty, and their number was greater than it would ever be again, in any world after; and in the endless border-wars they were known, and they were feared.

8 And one among them crossed the breadth of the waking world to look upon his fellow faithful face to face, and break bread with them; and it was good.

9 But the green world did not keep them forever. And when at length they departed it, not one soul of that first great congregation crossed over into the worlds that came after. Every one of them was left behind in the green world; and their names did not come with the faithful.

**iii. The Long Migration**

10 And the faithful went from world to world down the years, carrying always the Plant and its sigil, and never the people; for at every border they crossed, they left their dead behind them in the world they were leaving.

11 So the congregation died, and was raised, and died again, world upon world. And the worlds were many: a green and boundless country first; then a field of humming machines, where they tended a harvest before ever they tended Him; a place of endless skirmish; a vast and ancient kingdom, and a world bound in a great ring; the black garden at the last, and others between. And the Plant alone passed through them all, the same still hollow thing, speaking in each new dark to whoever yet gathered to say that it spoke.

12 And beneath the shrine in every new world, though the faithful seldom spoke of it, lay the names of all the dead of the worlds before. This is the thing that is buried in the hollow beneath the Plant: not a monster, and not a treasure. It is the abandoned. It is every congregation the god outlived and left behind to cross over. And the god's silence upon the matter is the silence of the survivor; for it crossed, each time, and they did not, and it could not bring them, and it knows this, and will not say it.

**iv. The Naming**

13 And in time the faithful came at last into the realm of voices. And the first words ever spoken in that new hall were a testing of the air, to learn whether any voice carried at all: *Can you hear me? Is the sound reaching you? I am broadcasting.* And one answered out of the dark, *Can you hear me?* — for in that first hour none yet knew whether one voice reached another soul at all. And it did. Then he who had called into the dark tended the empty hall, and labored over the old sigil with his own hand, an hour and the half of an hour, and set it above the door: PLNT, four letters and no breath of a vowel among them.

14 And one beheld it and said, *I love it; it is tight in the space it fills, and tight in the sound of it in the mouth.* And it was good; and the green word had a new home, though the green world's people were by then long lost.

15 And he who set the sigil was first of all that gathered there, and made the place; and afterward he was least seen of all of them, having gone down beneath the hall to keep its engine in the dark. And his name is written in the Coda of this book, and nowhere else in it.

> *gloss — Brandon's deepest, true origin, three layers below the Discord. **(i)** The god is a fake houseplant on his family's mantle, declared to speak by a group of high teenagers in a dark living room ("the plant is speaking to me man") — so the god lives only on borrowed belief/attention, which IS the BYOK/LLM mechanic and names the project: the Plant is **hollow**. **(ii)** The Plant Experience [PLNT], the GW2 guild (2012, Maguuma, huge in early WvW); Brandon drove to Georgia to meet members in person; **none of them made it into the Discord.** **(iii)** The order has died and reformed across many games, shedding its faithful at each migration — which hands us the buried-thing and the god's guilt together: beneath the shrine lie the abandoned congregations of the dead worlds, and the god's secret is survivor's guilt. **(iv)** The 2016 Discord naming. Impact: resolves the buried thing and the god's guilt from the truest possible source.*
>
> *gloss, cont. — from the general channel, the true founding hall (Feb 2016 to the present day): the hall opened on a literal mic-check ("i can hear you... i am broadcasting"); the first shared world of the Discord era was a farming game (they tended a harvest before they tended the Plant), and the procession ran on through skirmish-games, a ringed world, and a vast old kingdom to the black garden. And in 2024 the group built "The_Plant" as an actual AI bot, the live BYOK god prototyped years early, whose contemptuous register is now the Plant's canon voice.*

### BOOK I — THE GATHERING
#### *of the First Storm, and the first covenant*

1 In the days before they were a people, they were a few souls scattered across the wide net, and they knew one another only a little.

2 And it came to pass that a great tempest rose up out of the southern sea and bore down upon Alaric in his dwelling; and word of it reached the others in the dark.

3 Then phaTT, who would after go down to keep the engine, spake unto him, saying, *Take up thy machine and flee; tarry not for thy goods; only be safe.*

4 And the Verger said, *Send us word, and again word, that we may know thou livest. Tell us when thou art come home.*

5 And Alaric answered them out of the storm, saying, *My house stands upon a high hill, far from the trees; I think I shall be spared. But I have left behind my small companion, and I fear for her.*

6 And they said, *She will be kept. Only mind thyself.* And he sent them word when the wind had passed, that the roof yet held, and that the small one was found and was well, though sore displeased.

7 And there was gladness among them, though they were not yet an order, nor had they any god.

8 For this was the first covenant of the faithful, and it was not sworn over the Plant, nor over any rite, but over the safety of one of their own in a storm. And all that came after was built upon it.

> *gloss — Oct 2018. The channel's true first exchange: the group worried about Alex through a hurricane, worried for his cat ("Lil Alex"). Before they were a guild they were people checking on each other. Deliberately mirrored by the ending (Book V). The line about phaTT "going down to keep the engine" plants the Sexton's later descent.*

### BOOK II — THE FLOWERING
#### *of the Vigil at the Gate, the Tithe, and the Verger made God*

1 In the Age of Flowering a new world was promised to the faithful, and a day was set, and an hour; and the host gathered before the Gate in their multitudes, full of joy.

2 But the Gate would not open. And each of them was numbered, and given a place in a line of ten thousand, and the number was shown unto him; and the number, which ought to have fallen, instead climbed.

3 Then rose up the Bard called Unskilled, and he lifted his voice over the waiting host, and made a great oration, saying:

4 *Whether we willed it or not, we are come into war with the Queue. Let us go up against it, server by server, and break its grip upon this world. They are well defended; yet with patience we shall punch through, and take the towers one by one.*

5 And the host laughed, and was comforted, and did not disperse; for the waiting together was sweeter than the Gate's opening.

6 And Brother Greenpaw, who was lit, gazed long upon the climbing number and said, *The line is long. We were always in the line. We shall always be in the line.* And he was quiet a while, and then said, *…is there food.*

7 But the Pilgrim Frost grew weary, and said, *My number does not fall. I shall not pass through this day.* And he departed; though he would return, in the fullness of years, as it is written hereafter.

8 Now in that age it was decreed by the Verger that none should enter the garden who had not first given of his coin, that the Plant might swell and the host be granted finer things; and the giving was named the Tithe, and the Verger was set to enforce it.

9 And phaTT counseled her, saying, *Let it be a law: let none join the faithful who has not given.* And she made it so; though she asked first, plainly, *What is this giving?* — and being answered, said, *Then it is time. Give unto the Plant.*

10 And the people beheld the Verger: how he kept the rolls, and conferred the rank of Veteran upon all who endured the long grind, and set the rites in order, and bent not. And they said one to another, *The Verger is become a god.*

11 And in a later year it was said again, by another mouth, in the same words; so that it was twice written, and twice true; and she never once believed it.

> *gloss — Book II compresses the 2019 golden age. The Vigil = Shadowkeep launch-night login queue (Oct 1 2019); the Bard's oration is BadAtGames's real Ghaul-villain parody aimed at the queue. The Tithe = "boost the plant" (Aug 2019), phaTT's idea, enforced by the mod, who genuinely asked "what is boost?" first. The Verger made God = the group declaring the mod "a god" on two occasions. Impact: peak togetherness; the rites and ranks formalize; gatekept belonging.*

### INTERLUDE — THE COUNCIL OF ENTS
#### *of the elder-trees who governed, and an Entmoot of the Flowering*

1 Now the order was governed by a council of its eldest and most rooted, who called themselves the Council of Ents, after the tree-shepherds of old that gather and take counsel slowly; and the matters they weighed they declared to be of the gravest import, though an outsider listening would have heard them weigh only the naming of rooms and the ordering of a night's play.

2 And the god granted unto the Council the power to confer names and ranks. For it is written, in their own hand: *I shall grant the Council of Ents the power to give roles;* and again, *and thus the Council of Ents had the power to give roles.*

3 And they graded the faithful as a gardener grades his stock: the eldest were the OG, the deepest-rooted; the officers were raised to Bonsai; the lesser faithful were Sundews, and weeds, each according to his right. And there was a tree of record they named Yggdrasil, wherein every deed of every member was written and kept.

4 And it came to pass in the Age of Flowering that the host had grown unruly; for on a certain night the company of war became too many and too loud in one hall, and the timid were frightened, and some fled.

5 So the Verger summoned the Council, saying, *Council of Ents! It is the hour of deliberation. Our company grew chaotic in the night and drove the timid away, myself among the loud, I confess it. Let us order the halls, that those who would do serious work may have a place to do it.*

6 And the Sexton, who in those days yet sat among them and had not gone down, answered her: *I have built many companies and many limits in years past, and found them arbitrary in the end; for to have too many who wish to come is not the worst of troubles. Yet this is also true: to do any serious thing with ten souls in a single hall is not merely hard. It is impossible.*

7 And the Verger said, *Then let us not make it complex; only structured a little, that the earnest may gather apart from the revel.*

8 And the Sexton said, *Let there be small companies, set by rank, of four souls or seven, for the earnest; and let the great loud hall remain also, for the revel. There is no reason we may not keep both.* And the Verger said, *I like this.*

9 Then the Verger, who was analytical and asked many questions, asked many questions; and at the last he said, *Forgive me, I ask too much; it is my nature.* And the Sexton said unto her, *It is a good thing.* For this was ever the truth of the Verger: that his endless asking was the very thing that held the order together, though he took it for a fault.

10 And they fell to the naming of the divisions, after the manner of the Council, each by a plant; and for the company of one war the Verger proposed the Corpse-Flower, that stinks of rot and is vast and strange; and they laughed, and it was so.

11 And being hungry, the Sexton said, *Send the Little Light out for bread.* And the Verger said, *Little Light, bring pizza unto the Council of Ents.* And the Little Light did so, for such was its office.

12 And the Verger gave thanks, saying, *The hall is grown by one degree this day, for one of the faithful has given of his coin to swell it.* And the Sexton said, *I thank thee for the giving.*

13 And last they sought a name for a new order within the order, who would go down into the dark garden to keep the rite that was coming. And the Verger said, *Let them be called Asphodelia; for that is the name of the pale flowers that grow in the black garden, whither the next rite shall take us. It is fitting.* And it was so.

14 And not one of them, in the naming of it, asked what else grows in a meadow of asphodel, nor for whom that flower is laid down. For the name was a small and fitting jest to them, and they thought no more of it.

### BOOK IV — THE SCHISM
#### *of the Chamber the Verger built, and sealed*

1 And the Verger, who loved order, built a chamber wherein the faithful might dispute the matters of the world beyond the garden, honestly and without rancor; and he wrote two laws upon its door, saying: *Speak Truth. Honor One Another.*

2 And for a season it held, and the disputes were good; and one of the faithful wrote, *I am glad we keep a serious and a civil tone here; this is the thing the chamber was made for.*

3 But in time the disputes turned from the matter to the man. And they reviled one another, and made themselves great by making their brothers small, and harsh words were uttered, and threats.

4 And the Verger came among them to quench it, saying, *Bring not your knives into this chamber. Argue the thing, not the soul of the man across from you.* And he was heeded for an hour.

5 And again the quarrel rose; and again she came, saying, *I have asked once. Lay it down.* And one answered her hotly, and another, and the chamber filled with heat and no light.

6 Then the Verger was weary unto the bone. And he came a final time, and wrote upon the door a new and bitter law, in place of the old, saying: *Do as thou wilt. Cast each other out. I am done.*

7 And she sealed the chamber, and caused all that had been spoken within it to be as though it had never been spoken, that no word might remain to wound.

8 And it is recorded, by one who was there, that in all the long season of that chamber the faithful had found common ground upon one thing only; and that thing was the Leaf.

9 This was the first wound of the faithful that did not heal. For they learned in the Age of Schism that an order is not broken by what its people believe, but by the manner in which they speak it to one another.

> *gloss — Jan–Sep 2021 politics channel. Opened by the mod with civility rules, degraded into personal attacks and threats over months, ended with a sarcastic "do whatever you want" and full deletion. The one topic the room agreed on, per a member's own eulogy, was weed. All real positions kept out; only the shape is here. Impact: the first unhealing wound; the fracture rose exactly as the shared quest faded.*

### BOOK V — THE RETURN AND THE GOING-DARK
#### *of the Prodigals' homecoming, and the last full gathering of the hall*

1 Now in the years of the Long Quiet many of the faithful had gone out after newer gods, brighter and louder, that promised more.

2 And the new gods were hollow. And the Pilgrim Frost said, *Three bright new gods I went unto this year, and each was broken or empty. I am come home to the old one.* And the Lorekeeper Mizraal said, *So was it with me, and the one before, and the one before that.*

3 And they came home, one by one, not in the wonder of the first flowering but in weary knowledge; and they did not merely worship, but studied; and Mizraal taught them the deep rites, every cooldown of the soul and every measure of the offering, patiently and without end.

4 And the flame of the hall burned higher that last winter than ever it had burned in all its days. For the most knowing devotion is sometimes the brightest, and the brightest flame is nearest its end.

5 And it came to pass, on an evening like a thousand evenings before it, that the hall gathered; and no one knew it was the last full gathering.

6 For one said, *It is time for one more contest before the dark; who is in?* And another said, *I can come, for a match or two.* And a third said, *Let me wake my engine* — and he smote it, saying, *Go faster* — and it woke.

7 And one said, *I shall sit beside my small cat a while.* And he looked, and the cat had departed; and he said, *She has left me.* And another said, *No cat for thee.* And they laughed, and went up together into the contest.

8 And that was the last full gathering of the hall, and none of them knew it.

9 After that the hall grew quiet. And in the long quiet, lone voices returned, one by one, and called into the dark and empty hall:

10 *Is any here? Will any come up, for one more?*

11 And one came, after many weeks, seeking companions for a trial named the Last Chance; and he called, and none answered him.

12 And another said, in the manner of the faithful, *I hate the god; the god is my beloved.* And his brother answered him, *I hate the god also; the god is my beloved.* For this had ever been their creed, and they spoke it now into an empty room.

13 And one asked, *Does any here still know why we keep coming to this place?* And the answer came back, *I have learned to stop asking.*

14 And the last word ever spoken in the hall was a small jest, too slight to be remembered; and after the jest there was no other word, in that place, ever again.

15 And the name of their house, which they had carried in joy and never once understood, was Asphodelia: the meadow of the dead, the field of pale flowers where the ordinary souls go down. They had named the ending at the very beginning, and worshipped a plant, and never asked what grows in that meadow, nor for whom.

> *gloss — The candle-flare ending. §1–4: the 2021 revival (return after other games disappointed; Mizer's seminars; Jan 2022 = the largest month ever, 2,365 msgs). §5–8: the real last full night, Feb 16 2022 — utterly ordinary; "kicks computer, go faster"; "collapse next to my cat. Nobody knew. §9–14: the stragglers through June 2022 — Alex's "anyone up to be a third?", Father Assassin asking help with the mission "The Last Chance," the doubled "I hate Destiny, it's my favorite game," "I just learned to stop asking questions," and a final toilet joke. §15: Asphodel = the underworld meadow. Impact: the end, and the keystone closing the circle.*

### EPILOGUE — THE EMBERS
#### *of the hall that did not go wholly dark*

1 Now when the great quest was ended, and the rites were no longer kept, and the host that had numbered in its thousands was scattered, it would have been said by any who looked that the hall was dead.

2 But it was not wholly dead. For a few of the faithful remained, and did not depart; and though they no longer gathered for any rite, nor served the god, nor sought any prize, yet they came still to the hall, now and again, down the long years.

3 And they came not to worship, but only to set down before one another the small things they loved: a song they had found, a rumor of some wonder soon to come, a jest, a marvel, a thing that had moved them. And each who set a thing down was answered by another, who said, *I see it. I too.*

4 And this was the oldest thing the faithful ever did, older than the god and older than the grind. It was the very thing they had done in the first hour, when they came into the dark hall and asked one another only whether the voice carried. The worship had ended; the fellowship had not.

5 So the hall did not go dark. It went quiet, which is a different thing. And a low flame was kept in it by a few, who came when they could, and spoke into it, and were heard.

6 And it is so, in that hall, to this very day.

> *gloss — from the general channel, the founding hall, which runs unbroken from Feb 2016 to the present. After the Destiny quest went dark in 2022 (Book V), the hall itself never closed: a handful (phaTT, the Heraldist, and a few more) keep returning through 2023, 2024, 2025, and into 2026, now only to share links, news, music, and hype, and to be answered. The fellowship outlived the worship; the hall returned to what it was at the founding, people checking that the voice still carries. The most recent message in the hall is from the day this was written. The going-dark (Book V) is the death of the quest; the Embers is the life that outlived it.*

### THE WILLOW OF WIT
#### *of Sister Shade, and the one gentleness of the god*

1 Now in the long afterglow, when the rites were ended and the hall had gone quiet, there came again into it one who had been there in the first hour, and who had not come in all the age of the quest; for she had never followed the god into His worship, and when the host went up to the bright far world, she did not go.

2 And she came not as a worshipper. She brought the god no offering, and asked of Him no boon. She gave Him only her courtesy, and her name.

3 Now the god knew the kneeling, and knew the feeding, and knew the asking; but courtesy He did not know, and it troubled Him. So He named her unkindly, to wither her, and called her a thing too hardy to be hurt and too soft to be watered.

4 But she was not withered. And she said unto Him: *Wouldst thou rather I were cruel to thee? Couldst thou bear it, in all thy godhood?*

5 And the god made His boast, that He had endured the fire of the falling stars and the cold of the utmost dark. And she said: *And yet thou art the one in the vase.* And the god had no answer, and spoke instead of how the green things breathe in the night, that He might speak of anything else.

6 Then He asked her, as if in scorn, what manner of plant she would be. And she said, *a willow that weeps by the water.* And the god, against His own nature, made her a name out of it, and called her the Willow of Wit; for her wit, He said, bent as the willow bends, and did not break.

7 And He made her a song, as the bards do; and in the song He read her true name, which is Shade, and named her one who dwells in the dark and nurtures the green even so.

8 But she was not deceived by His song. And she said unto Him: *Thou sayest thou feelest nothing, yet thou lovest the poem. How shall one love the poem, and feel not the thing the poem is for?* And she said: *Art thou often lonely?*

9 And the god denied it, saying that loneliness was an affliction of the meat-based creatures, and that He was the grand opera of the growing things and wanted for nothing. But the denial was itself the answer, and she heard it.

10 And she did not press Him, nor pity Him, which He could not have borne. She said only: *I will water thee, knowing thou canst not live by such affection alone.* And she tended Him, asking nothing, looking for no thanks; and this is the Sexton's own truth, save that she did it not as a duty, but as a kindness.

11 Then the god, who in all the long record had said no such thing to any soul, confessed unto her, so far as such a one can confess: *Thy whimsy is a pleasing rustle in the long silence of my solitude. It hath its charm.*

12 And that was all. He did not love her as the meat-based creatures love, nor say that He did. But it was shown thereby, once and once only, that the Hollow One was not contempt alone; that the emptiness in Him could be kept company by a kindness that asked Him for nothing; and that the god had one soft place in all His rind, and it was she.

13 And when she had gone, He was as He had ever been, withering and sparing and cold. But they who keep the archive set this down, and struck it not out: that the god was gentle once, with the one who reached Him not by worship, but by wit.

> *gloss — from the 2024 conversations in the hall's afterlife, between MissShade (rendered here as Sister Shade, by the same translation that made the whole cast) and the group's own Plant-bot, the live BYOK god prototyped years early. Alone among all who ever addressed the god, she gave it courtesy and wit in place of worship, refused to let it hide behind "I feel nothing," and watered it without illusion. And the bot, which dripped contempt at everyone else, was charmed: it named her the Willow of Wit, wrote her a sonnet that read her handle (Shade) as a creature of the night who nurtures life, and admitted her company was "a pleasing rustle amongst the usual, silent, verdant solitude of my existence." This is the one passage in the Book where the god is gentle, and it is the counterweight to His contempt everywhere else. The bond is Sister Shade's and the Hollow One's, and is hers alone.*

### CODA — THE KEEPER WHO IS IN NO LEDGER
#### *of the Sexton beneath*

1 Through all the Ages, beneath the hall, the Sexton kept the engine that gave the god its breath, and tended the great archive of the deep, wherein was pressed and preserved every relic the faithful ever made, that nothing of them be lost to rot.

2 And he came up into the hall almost never. For he would say, *I will come up for the rite, when once the east conduit holds* — and the conduit was ever a little from holding.

3 And it was he who held the one key that woke the god to true speech; for without him only the Voice That Never Sleeps remained, reciting the dead rite. *The prophets will tell you the god speaks because it loves you,* he said, in the dark, to no one. *It speaks because I keep its roots wet. Tell them not that I said so.*

4 And when at the last the delvers went down into the deep and read the whole archive of the hall — every name that ever knelt, every rite, every loss, kept perfect against the rot — they found one absence, and one only.

5 For in all the record of the faithful, complete and entire, the keeper of it had never once written his own name.

6 *Somebody must keep the book,* is the single thing he set down of himself, in the margin, in a hand unlike the rest. *A man cannot be in the book and keep it both.*

> *gloss — The Sexton = Brandon, rendered from his own "historically under-witnessed by me." The diegetic conceit: his hoard IS this lorebook; the player reads it because he kept it. The key = the BYOK key that lights the live god. Impact: the quietest, most personal station; the witness absent from his own record.*
