# THE HOLLOW: The Book of the Plant, and the Hollow God

**Date:** June 29, 2026 (running into the small hours of the 30th)
**Participants:** Brandon Kelly (Board / Director), Claude (Opus, design & writing partner)
**Artifacts:** `the-hollow-book-of-the-plant.md`, the in-world scripture, carried from first draft to **v1.0 standalone**; `the-hollow-lorebook-notes.md`, the working bible, to **v1.0 standalone**; throwaway parser and mining scripts over the raw channel exports

---

## 1. High-Level Summary

This session built the lorebook for [[The Hollow]] out of the real fourteen-year history of [[The Plant]], and in the course of it found the origin beneath every other origin. The work moved across one context compaction. Before the compaction: mine the Destiny channel, the politics and song-request channels, and Brandon's tweet archive; set the governing approach (a dying convent, told as modern internet-age fables); measure the community's real activity curve; draft the Book of the Plant and a working notes bible; and render the operator, Brandon himself, as the Sexton beneath the world. After the compaction: mine the founding Council of Ents channel and a dated raids-and-movie-nights ledger, then receive the deepest true origin and a photograph of the actual object, which reorganized the whole cosmology and closed the two lore holes that had been left open. The session ended by stripping both documents to clean, standalone canon.

The headline is that the true origin unified the project. The Plant is a real, physical, fake houseplant that a few high teenagers in a dark living room declared was speaking; so the god is hollow, alive only on borrowed belief, which is at once the project's name, the BYOK live-god mechanic stated as theology, and the literal sacred object of Brandon's adolescence, all one thing. And the Guild Wars 2 first age, the largest the community ever was and none of whom crossed into the Discord, handed over the two unknowns the lore had been missing: the thing buried beneath the shrine is the abandoned dead of the prior worlds, and the Plant's guilt is the survivor's. The lorebook went from a partial draft asserting a cosmology to two self-contained documents holding a whole one.

## 2. Work Done / Topics

### Before the compaction: the mine, the spine, and the Sexton

**The source, parsed and read.** Built HTML parsers over the DiscordChatExporter dumps and read the Destiny channel end to end (17,155 messages, Oct 2018 to Jun 2022), plus the politics channel, the song-request channel, and Brandon's 3,177-tweet archive. The exports were treated as scripture-source, not transcript: keep the shape, discard the proper nouns.

**The governing approach, locked.** The lore's theme is the dying convent, a community that is also a church and goes quiet, honest about the silence after. Its form is the modern fable, Aesop by way of Discord, goofy and grieving in the same breath. Its sourcing rule is translate the spirit, not the letter, governed by a no-gloss test: if a fable needs a Destiny footnote to land, it is cut. The Doobie treatment governs the cast: real members reimagined as fantasy creatures, never literal.

**The spine, measured not assumed.** The activity curve disproved a gentle-taper reading and gave a candle-flare instead: founding spark late 2018, first golden age Oct 2019 (Shadowkeep, 1,367 messages), a muffled 2020, near-death in early 2021 (April, nine messages), a great revival, the single largest month ever in Jan 2022 (2,365), then sudden dark (March through June: 1, 1, 2, 5). The two fires differ in kind: 2019 was naive launch-night anticipation, 2021 was weary expert devotion, prodigals who tried other gods and came home to master the old rite. The five movements became the lorebook's structure.

**The cast and the keystone.** The core trio set: the Plant (the rationed contempt-god), Brother Greenpaw (from DoobieSnacks, the stoner-mystic), and the Warden (from Luna/Zez, the order-keeper). A roster of directions drawn from the logs behind them. The keystone identified: the real clan name Asphodelia, asphodel being the flower of the Greek underworld, the meadow of the ordinary dead, which meant the ending had been encoded in the group's own name all along.

**The Sexton.** Brandon's own tweets and posts were read closely and rendered into the one character beneath the world: the keeper of the machinery and the archive below the shrine, a badger of the deep sett, holder of the one key that lights the living god. Built from the evidence, the builder of apparatus, the archivist who distrusts erasure, the elegist for old web community, the conditional presence with an exit in every yes, the prolific writer who calls himself quiet. His crack is exact: he preserves everything because he cannot be present for anything, and his own name appears in none of the records he keeps.

**The two deliverables.** The Book of the Plant (Chronicle, Voices, the Book proper from Genesis to Coda, every scene carrying a for-our-eyes factual gloss underneath the scripture) and the lorebook notes (the working bible of approach, cast, worldbuilding, and a numbered catalogue of fable seeds).

### After the compaction: the Council, the ledger, and the origin beneath the origin

**The Council of Ents, mined.** Brandon supplied the founding channel (May 2016 to June 2023, the oldest and longest-running text yet) and a small recent voice channel (2022 to 2026, the remnant after Destiny). The parser was extended for the newer export format. The channel resolved the name's origin: on May 15 2016, Brandon made a logo in Photoshop and named the channel **PLNT**, four letters and no vowel; a member's reply, "I love PLNT, it's tight both in the space it uses and what it sounds like," is on record. The operator named the god. It also delivered the order's governance whole: the **Council of Ents** as the officer council, with botanical ranks (Bonsai, Sundews, weeds, OG, a proposed Hydroponic), an audit-log channel named **Yggdrasil**, "Ent Moots" for meetings, and the group's own verbatim scripture-voice for their admin ("I shall grant the Council of Ents the power to give roles, god somewhere"). They had been writing the Book's register themselves. And **Asphodelia** was sourced precisely: chosen by the Council in Aug 2019 as "the flowers that grow in the black garden," because the next raid was set in Destiny's Black Garden, the Destiny meaning on top and the underworld meaning underneath.

**The ledger, folded in.** Brandon's real "PLANT PAST EVENTS" table (dated raids and movie nights with rosters) became the Chronicle's spine of recorded gatherings: the recurring Garden of Salvation, a Raid of Thanks at Thanksgiving, a Polar Express at midwinter, day-one descents with the two fireteams named Maple and Evergreen, the Beholdings of the last bright year. Themes became dated, witnessed events.

**The Book advanced (v0.2).** Added Book Zero (the Naming), a Council of Ents interlude with a fully rendered Entmoot scene (the founding-of-roles meeting as multi-character dialogue, the Warden and the Sexton deciding the order's structure, the naming of Asphodelia), the Ledger of Gatherings, and two new voices, the Heraldist (from T-Unit93, the sigil-maker, witness to the 2016 naming) and Cody the Tally-keeper (the recorder of deeds). Notes brought to v0.5.

**The true origin, received.** Brandon then gave the deepest canon and a photograph of the object. The Plant is a real fake houseplant that sat on his family's mantle; as teenagers, high, in the dark, one said "the plant is speaking to me, man," and they all believed, and so it spoke. Before that, the community formed in 2012 in Guild Wars 2 as "The Plant Experience [PLNT]," one of the biggest guilds in Maguuma's border wars; Brandon drove to Georgia to meet members in person; and none of that first, largest congregation crossed into the Discord. The order has died and reformed across games for over a decade, carrying the sigil and never the people.

**The cosmology, unified and the holes closed (v0.3).** This resolved the project's deepest questions from the truest source. The god is hollow, alive only on borrowed belief, which names the project and is the BYOK mechanic exactly, and the going-dark is not the god dying but reverting to an object when no one is left to say it speaks. The thing buried beneath the shrine is the abandoned dead of the prior worlds; the Plant's guilt is survivor's guilt, that it crossed each time and they did not. Genesis was rewritten into four movements (the First Speaking, the Green World, the Long Migration, the Naming); an Age Before the Ages was added to the Chronicle; and a visual-canon section was written from the photograph: the Plant hero and the "vase" hero are one object, a dried arrangement in a pewter funerary urn, and the shrine's architecture is a hearth, mantel-altar above, hollow and ash below, flue to the surface. Notes brought to v0.6.

**The standalone cleanup.** Both documents were stripped to clean canon. The Book lost its production-tracking section, its version archaeology, and its frontmatter links to the other files, so it reads as pure scripture with glosses and ends on the Coda. The notes were rewritten: changelog phrasing and dated status lines removed, the fable catalogue reconciled into a single run of 1 through 17 (the three true-origin stations folded in, the Sexton's fable settled as #17), the operator's NPC question retired as resolved, and the old to-do checklist replaced by a plain Settled / Open / Still-to-render status. Verified both ways: neither file references the other, and a stale-marker sweep came back empty. Both went to v1.0 standalone, the Book to a versionless canonical filename.

## 3. Insights & Takeaways

- **The true origin is the kind of coherence you cannot design toward, only find.** Theme, title, core mechanic, and the literal sacred object of Brandon's adolescence turned out to be a single thing: a fake plant that speaks only because people say it does. The Hollow was always the name for the god.
- **The two open lore holes were answered from the source, not invented.** The buried thing (the abandoned dead of the prior worlds) and the Plant's guilt (survivor's, for crossing and leaving them) both fall straight out of "none of the GW2 guild made it into the Discord." They are recommended as canon, kept veiled in-game.
- **The community had already written the Book's register.** "I shall grant the Council of Ents the power to give roles, god somewhere" is theirs, verbatim. The job was transcription as much as invention; the botanical ranks, the Yggdrasil audit-log, and the Entmoots were sitting in the founding channel.
- **The convent has always been dying.** The Destiny going-dark is not the first death; it is the latest in a decade of migrations that shed the faithful at every border. That recontextualizes the whole arc and gives the ending its floor.
- **The Sexton is the genesis, not a late addition.** The one who keeps the engine below is the one who named the world in 2016. Creation and the boiler room are the same hands, which is the truest thing the lore says about its author.
- **Standalone is a property worth enforcing.** Glosses and a working bible are features, but production scaffolding, version churn, and cross-document pointers are not; pulling them out is what lets each file stand on its own.

## 8. Tone & Sentiment

**Discovery-led, and it kept paying out.** Each upload opened a deeper floor: the founding channel gave the name and the governance, the ledger gave the dated rites, and then the photograph and the true origin gave the whole cosmology its spine. The session's emotional center was the moment the fake plant on a family mantle turned out to be the project's title, its mechanic, and its god at once.

**Faithful to the disciplines.** The no-gloss test, the spirit-not-letter sourcing, and the veiling of the buried thing held throughout; the verbatim survivors stayed rare and earned; the excluded material (the politics-channel content, a handful of off-theme memes) stayed excluded, with only the shape of the fracture kept.

**The build, then the broom.** The creative work was warm and high; the closing cleanup was pure execution, asked for plainly and delivered without ceremony. The lorebook ends the session as two clean standalone documents, a Book that reads as scripture and a bible that holds the canon, with a short, honest list of what is still to be written.
